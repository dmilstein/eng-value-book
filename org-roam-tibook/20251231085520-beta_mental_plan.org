#+title: Beta Mental Plan
Part of [[id:9410A7DC-111C-4791-B8A1-C20867904251][Unstuck Beta]], and more broadly [[id:22AA9654-9394-40C6-8D9E-C725D9504974][Beta Reading]].

* Journaling <2025-12-31 Wed>
Okay, so I've just launched my second beta (first of the new book). And, within a brisk 3 hours, I went from excited and accomplished to anxious and fearful.

Let's take a moment to slow down and notice and appreciate this! The human experience is delightful and fascinating.

And I knew this would come.

I think I want to meet this moment, both mentally and with habits.

** Mentally: *Enjoy It*

Relish the fear!

This is a marvelous chance to learn. If I can find a way to be excited to be in the unknown, I'll have an incredible advantage for the rest of my writing career. This is the start of a *career*, not evidence of potential disaster. I mean, for god's sakes, I'm clearly an incredible writer, just like I'm an incredible director, but I only ever make truly great plays by relentlessly showing them to people and improving them. My ability to do that is what makes me great. The skill is there, but the process is key.

Laugh! And take pride in being able to face it!

Years from now, with multiple best-selling books under my belt, I'm going to look back on this moment with pride. Let that story grow in my head. Even if I take a full-time job on, nothing will stop me from getting these books out into the world.

Keep the visualization of the invited run through at the showplace in my head. My own eager curiosity to hear from people, with my total confidence that we'll end up making something truly great, if we can keep working and getting feedback.

That's what I'm doing. There is no failure.

** Habits: *Operationalize It*

Maybe it's limits, or maybe it's a defined process? Start tracking... what?

I assume that people will bail out at a certain point. My prediction is... what?

First off, set goals. 25% of invites turning into readers within 7 days (14?).

So if I send out 12 invites, that's 3 readers. Don't feel hurt!

Only worry if legit no one even follows the links -- but so far, that's not happening.

I've been good and disciplined about the content of the email.

For this very first round of beta invites, almost my only question is:

*When do they stop reading, on average?"

My prediction is that, of those 3 readers:

 - 1 will read the whole thing

 - 2 will bail within 3-5 chapters

So I'm working on designing my next *test*. Which is to figure out how to hook people more.

I've already got evidence that it's an important problem. And I probably have a good amount of what I need written.

In an ideal world, aim for another round of beta reading on Feb 3rd. Yes, I like that. In 4 weeks.

On Monday <2026-01-12 Mon>, I'll decide what I've learned, and spend a few weeks getting ready for another round of Beta Reading.

Should I try to control my desire to check it? I dunno. It's so tempting. Maybe let myself indulge for a bit, and commit to observing.

Oooh, what if I say I've got a journal. And I have to write in it, every time I check?

That's good. A mini retro each time.
